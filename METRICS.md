### 2.2 Confusion pattern — what the LLM proposed instead, when wrong

Two systematic biases, not random noise:

**Bias A — "customer-side card issue" misread as "nothing can be done."**
Policy says `payment_link_nudge` (get the customer to act); model said `no_action_unrecoverable` instead, every single time it was wrong on these codes:
- `card_expired`: 22/22 wrong cases → `no_action_unrecoverable`
- `card_disabled_for_online_payments`: 7/7 wrong cases → `no_action_unrecoverable`
- `debit_instrument_inactive`: 2/2 wrong cases → `no_action_unrecoverable`
- `incorrect_cvv`: 1/1 wrong case → `no_action_unrecoverable`

**Bias B — "any technical or unclear decline" misread as "wait and retry later."**
Policy says either `immediate_retry` or `payment_link_nudge`; model said `delayed_retry` instead, every single time it was wrong on these codes:
- `payment_timed_out` (correct: `immediate_retry`): 5/5 wrong cases → `delayed_retry`
- `gateway_technical_error` (correct: `immediate_retry`): 4/4 wrong cases → `delayed_retry`
- `bank_technical_error` (correct: `immediate_retry`): 1/1 wrong case → `delayed_retry`
- `authentication_failed` (correct: `payment_link_nudge`): 11/11 wrong cases → `delayed_retry`
- `payment_failed` (correct: `payment_link_nudge`): 5/5 wrong cases → `delayed_retry`
- `card_declined` (correct: `payment_link_nudge`): 7/10 wrong cases → `delayed_retry`; 2/10 → `no_action_unrecoverable`; 1/10 → `no_action_fraud`

## 5. Gate hard-block behavior

Per `gate.py`: `MAX_ACTION_AMOUNT_PAISE` = ₹50,000/action, `MAX_RUN_TOTAL_PAISE`
= ₹5,00,000/run.

- **Subscription-recovery run:** 0 hard blocks. Every one of the 150 synthetic
  records was well under the per-action cap, and the run total (₹144,253.79
  executed) never approached ₹5,00,000. This means the cap has **never
  fired** in the subscription pipeline — it's implemented and unit-tested
  (`tests/test_gate.py`), but not demonstrated against real batch data.
- **Route demo run (`route_demo.py`), separate scenario:** 5 transfers
  attempted, **1 blocked** — `sub_route_demo_004`, reason logged verbatim
  as *"Amount 550000.00 exceeds per-action cap of 50000.00"* — an
  attempted ₹5,50,000.00 transfer correctly blocked against the
  ₹50,000.00 per-action cap. This is the only real evidence in the whole
  project of the spending cap actually intercepting an oversized action —
  worth highlighting in the pitch over the subscription pipeline's
  0-blocks number, which only proves the cap didn't get in the way, not
  that it works.

## 6. Idempotency

**Untested at the only scale that matters.** No `subscription_id` repeats
across the 150 synthetic records by construction (`generate_data.py`
generates unique IDs), so the idempotency check has never fired outside
`tests/test_gate.py`'s unit tests. `RESULTS.md`'s "Hard-blocked: 0" should
not be read as proof idempotency works under real conditions — it's
absence of a scenario that would have exercised it, not a passed test at
scale. This is disclosed in `BUILD_LOG.md` §12; repeated here since it
directly affects how the "0 hard-blocked" number should be interpreted.

## 8. What these numbers do and don't prove

- **Do prove:** the gate is load-bearing (46% override rate is not
  negligible), the audit log is complete and internally consistent (every
  count here ties out exactly to `RESULTS.md`'s independently-written
  summary), the model has specific, nameable failure patterns rather than
  random noise (§2.2).
- **Don't prove:** real-world revenue recovery (everything is simulated),
  the spending cap working under real load (0 fires in 150 records), or
  idempotency working under real load (0 duplicate IDs in the test set).
